# Zing Protocol

The internet gave creators a megaphone. Platforms gave them an audience. But somewhere along the way, the deal turned extractive.

Google built a search empire on the back of independent publishers — then trained AI on their writing and stopped sending them traffic. YouTube takes 45% of ad revenue. Instagram owns your follower list behind API. TikTok decides who sees your work based on what keeps people scrolling, not what you created.

**This isn't a bug. It's the business model.**

Zing is building something different: a creator economy where the platform is infrastructure, not a landlord. In the Zing model, the creator owns three things that Web2 platforms have always kept for themselves: the content itself (stored permanently on Walrus, immovable and uncensorable), the audience relationship (member data is on-chain, portable, not locked in any platform), and the revenue stream (fans pay in USDC directly, no intermediary taking 30% and exchange friction).

The three shifts Zing makes:

- **From rented to owned** — Your content is a on-chain asset. It exists whether Zing exists or not.
- **From algorithmic to direct** — Your fans subscribe directly. There is no algorithm between a creator and their revenue.
- **From extractive to sovereign** — Your content is not a locked asset. Download it anytime, build visualizations, or publish to third-party platforms. Your data, your rules.
